01

Executive summary

One brand ecosystem.
Two distinct purposes.

Faith & Eve creates expressive, durable clothing for girls ages 5–13 and turns every customer relationship into an invitation to widen access for other children.

The commercial company designs and sells premium, small-batch apparel inspired by vintage silhouettes and made for modern life. A separately governed nonprofit supports clothing access, repair and reuse, and community-based creative programs.

North starMake heirloom-quality clothing desirable, useful, and part of a more generous system.
02

Legal structure

Designed for
clarity and trust.

For-profit

Faith & Eve, LLC

Owns the brand, designs products, manages inventory, offers photography services, employs the commercial team, and operates retail and wholesale channels.

  • Revenue from products and photography sessions
  • Responsible for product, session, and customer operations
  • May make documented charitable gifts
Nonprofit

Faith & Eve Foundation

A separately governed charitable organization that receives donations and runs mission programs for children and families.

  • Independent board and bank accounts
  • Separate books, contracts, and reporting
  • Donation receipts issued by the nonprofit

The entities will use written agreements for any shared staff, services, data, trademarks, or facilities. Tax-deductible language will be used only after the nonprofit has the required tax-exempt status, and only for eligible contributions.

05

Checkout experience

Make generosity
simple, never assumed.

At checkout, customers see a clearly optional contribution to the Faith & Eve Foundation.

  1. Choose no gift, round up, or select a fixed amount.
  2. The donation appears as a separate line item.
  3. The nonprofit sends a receipt and explains deductibility.
  4. Orders are never delayed or treated differently based on giving.

06

Promotion strategy

Launch a chapter,
not just a catalog.

Promote Faith & Eve as a small, cinematic world for families who want fewer, better clothes—not as another children’s boutique competing on volume or discounts.

Launch platformClothes to grow stories in.

Every campaign should show a garment in a real childhood moment: the muddy knee, the Sunday table, the woodland walk, the piece passed along.

01 · Hero story

The First Winter

Release the eight-piece assortment as one tightly edited chapter. Lead with the Elowen Coat, Sunday Cable, Eve Dress, and Story Cardigan; merchandise the remaining pieces as complete looks and useful layers.

02 · Owned audience

Notes from Faith & Eve

Turn the existing newsletter into the primary launch list. Offer early access, studio notes, fit guidance, care stories, and first notice of small restocks—useful intimacy rather than constant promotions.

03 · Visual discovery

Instagram & Pinterest

Create vertical films, detail studies, outfit boards, and saveable “one piece, three ways” styling. Pinterest supports the nostalgic planning behavior; Instagram carries motion, founder voice, and parent-creator storytelling.

04 · Earned trust

Small creator seeding

Gift selectively to parent stylists, family photographers, slow-fashion voices, and vintage-interior creators whose taste already fits the brand. Prioritize reusable content rights and genuine fit feedback over follower count.

05 · Search demand

Shoppable product data

Build full product pages with fiber, measurements, care, shipping, returns, and descriptive image alt text. Feed approved inventory into Google Merchant Center free listings before adding paid search.

06 · Physical proof

Storybook pop-ups

Pair one-day trunk shows and seasonal portrait appointments with aligned bookstores, family cafés, holiday markets, and independent boutiques. Let families feel fabrics, verify sizing, meet the photographer, and join the launch list.

6–8 weeks beforeGather

Produce the campaign with the photography partner, publish the founder story, open the waitlist and session inquiries, seed samples, and share material and fit previews.

Launch weekReveal

Give email subscribers first access, release the full editorial story, host a small try-on event, and retarget engaged visitors.

Weeks 2–8Prove

Publish customer styling, fit answers, repair and care content, low-stock truth, and the first transparent donation update.

Before promotion: replace or license every current editorial and product image; the repository identifies them as prototype references only. Verify fiber, origin, sustainability, safety, inventory, and impact claims before publishing. Keep the nonprofit contribution optional and separate from product value, and never imply the entire purchase is tax-deductible.

Paid or gifted creator relationships must be disclosed clearly where the endorsement appears. Secure written usage rights before reposting or turning creator material into ads.

Measure weekly

Waitlist growth · email conversion · product-page engagement · add-to-cart rate · cost per first purchase · sell-through by SKU and size · return reasons · repeat purchase · donation participation

07

Intellectual property

Protect what makes
the brand recognizable.

Preliminary screenNo obvious exact match identified
Attorney review required before filing or launch

The priority asset is the Faith & Eve name, supported by its wordmark, logo, original artwork, garment designs, photography, copy, patterns, and customer-facing brand system.

01

Clear the name

Search FAITH & EVE, FAITH AND EVE, phonetic equivalents, spacing and punctuation variants, dominant-word combinations, and marks with a similar meaning or commercial impression.

02

File strategically

After clearance, consider a standard-character application for the name and a separate application for the logo, covering the goods and services actually used or supported by a bona fide intent to use.

03

Own the work

Use written IP-assignment and work-made-for-hire terms with founders, employees, photographers, illustrators, patternmakers, agencies, and contractors. Preserve dated source files and approvals.

04

Maintain & enforce

Track filing and renewal deadlines, monitor confusingly similar uses, document first use, use ™ before registration, and use ® only after federal registration for the covered goods or services.

Review performed August 15, 2026. A preliminary exact-name review did not reveal an obvious federal match in publicly searchable results. This is not a legal clearance opinion and does not establish availability. The USPTO warns that marks can conflict when they are similar in sound, appearance, meaning, or commercial impression and used with related goods or services—even across different classes.

Before adoption or filing, a U.S.-licensed trademark attorney should complete a comprehensive search of live and relevant dead federal records, state registrations, business names, domains, social platforms, marketplaces, and unregistered/common-law uses. Review should focus initially on apparel, online retail, charitable fundraising, and related nonprofit services.

08

36-month roadmap

Build carefully.
Prove what works.

0–6 months

Foundation

Finalize entity formation, governance, trademark and intercompany agreements. Validate fit, pricing, suppliers, and the first collection.

6–18 months

Launch

Release the direct-to-consumer shop, pilot checkout giving, publish impact reporting, and build repeat purchase.

18–36 months

Expansion

Add selective wholesale, repair and resale, nonprofit program partners, and measured seasonal growth.

Scorecard

Track sell-through, gross margin, return rate, repeat purchase, garments redistributed, program participation, donation conversion, and dollars delivered to mission.

The operating principle

Commerce and charity can reinforce one another—when the line between them is unmistakably clear.
